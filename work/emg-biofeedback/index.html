EMG biofeedback training system

A real-time system that turns a patient's own muscle activity into a cursor in a reaching game, plus the muscle-synergy analysis pipeline behind it. Both are used in the lab's clinical experiments.

October 2024 – April 2026 · Research assistant (part-time), NeuRRo Lab, University of Michigan · MATLAB, Python/PyTorch, Unity, C#

My scope

I built the real-time acquisition and control loop, the on-device optimizer port, and the MATLAB synergy pipeline. The experiments and the clinical questions are the lab's; nothing participant-level appears on this page.

Why this is a real-time problem

Biofeedback rests on one move: make the muscle itself the controller. Activity from the arm drives a cursor, the cursor has to reach a target, and effort that would otherwise be invisible becomes something on screen that either moves or does not.

Which turns a clinical idea into a real-time engineering problem. Between electrode and cursor sit band-pass filtering, envelope extraction, EMA smoothing and an MVC calibration, all of which have to happen fast enough that the cursor feels like a limb rather than a video playback — and stable enough that noise does not read as intent.

What I built

  • The real-time loop. Acquisition of up to 16 EMG channels at 2 kHz mapped to on-screen cursor control in both position and velocity modes, with band-pass filtering, envelope extraction, EMA smoothing and MVC calibration to keep noise from reading as movement.
  • An 11-stage experiment protocol built into the software, so a session runs the same way every time without an operator remembering the order.
  • The optimizer port. A gradient-descent optimizer with a multi-objective loss — target error, channel balance, regularization — hand-written from PyTorch into Unity C#, removing the external dependency so the decoder adapts on-device inside the game loop.
  • The synergy pipeline. Roughly a thousand lines of MATLAB implementing an NNMF–NNR framework to extract muscle synergies from EMG and track how they co-evolve across gait conditions, with correlation-based alignment holding synergies in correspondence across participants.

Results & boundary

  • Both the biofeedback system and the synergy pipeline are used in the lab's clinical experiments — not demos.
  • The synergy pipeline produced between-group statistics and visualizations across 18 participants, as objective measures for evaluating treatment effect.
  • 18 months of continuous work on one system, October 2024 to April 2026, across both the real-time loop and the offline pipeline.

Further thoughts

The decision I am proudest of sounds like the least impressive one: I hand-wrote the optimizer in C# instead of calling it from Python. Not for speed as an end in itself. A biofeedback loop runs while a participant is sitting in front of it, and every external process inside that loop is one more thing that can stall at the worst possible moment. Taking the dependency out meant the decoder could adapt on-device, inside the game loop, with nothing to wait on. The “why” there tells you more about how I make decisions than the port does — and porting an optimizer by hand is only possible if you understand what its loss is actually computing, which is the part I would want to be asked about.

What I cannot give you is the number that would prove any of it. I do not have latency or frame-rate figures for the ported decoder, before or after. It held up in sessions, and “it held up” is not a measurement. For a real-time system that is exactly the wrong way round: the timing harness should have been the first commit.

The harder problem was in the analysis. Pulling synergies out of one participant is a factorization; making them comparable across eighteen is the actual work, because the factorization returns its components in no fixed order or orientation. I used correlation-based alignment to hold them in correspondence, and I still think that step deserves more scrutiny than it got — it quietly decides what “the same synergy” means before any statistics are run on top of it.
